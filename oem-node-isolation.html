Protocol 02 // Production-Node Governance

OEM Node Isolation

Design outsourced production so that no single operating node silently owns the whole product logic.

Direct answer

OEM Node Isolation is a production-architecture method for separating critical knowledge, processing responsibilities, and change control across the manufacturing system. It reduces avoidable concentration while preserving the information and evidence needed to govern the finished product.

When it applies

Master-OEM concentration

One partner holds formula handling, critical conversion, final assembly, and release influence at the same time.

Know-how exposure

A commercially sensitive formula, pre-mix, process sequence, or supplier relationship needs clearer access boundaries.

Transfer planning

The business needs a credible alternative node without creating uncontrolled product drift.

Change-control ambiguity

It is unclear which technical, material, or scheduling changes must be visible before they affect the product.

How the method works

  1. Identify the product knowledge and process steps that are truly critical to identity, safety, stability, or differentiation.
  2. Separate responsibility where it is operationally sensible: for example, controlled pre-mix, component supply, conversion, final assembly, or release.
  3. Define the evidence that must travel between nodes without disclosing more than the role requires.
  4. Build change-control and transfer rules before commercial urgency forces a rushed handoff.

Usable takeaway

Do not ask only who makes the product. Ask which node can change it, what it is allowed to know, and what evidence must be reviewed before that change reaches the market.

Scope boundary

Node isolation is not a legal IP filing, a secrecy guarantee, or a recommendation to fragment every supply chain. The objective is proportionate governance: protect critical interfaces while retaining traceability, quality control, and commercially workable execution.
