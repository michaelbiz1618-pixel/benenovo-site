Protocol 03 // Commercial Execution Readiness

Scale-Up Readiness

Test whether the operating system can preserve the intended product outcome as volume, nodes, and market exposure increase.

Direct answer

Scale-Up Readiness reviews the interfaces that commonly fail between a viable product concept and repeatable commercial execution: product requirements, material and process conditions, release controls, production capacity, and cross-border continuity. It identifies what must be evidenced before scale adds pressure.

When it applies

First commercial run

A product is moving from bench, pilot, or limited supply into a formal commercial manufacturing window.

Factory or market transfer

The manufacturing node, equipment profile, supply base, packaging, or market is changing.

Post-launch drift

Yield, sensory, stability, release, or channel performance is deviating from the intended product outcome.

Capacity step-up

Volume, line pressure, scheduling, or supplier substitution is increasing faster than the evidence base.

How the method works

  1. Define the acceptance boundary for the finished product, not only the input specification.
  2. Map the process, material, packaging, release, storage, and channel conditions that can move the outcome outside that boundary.
  3. Compare the next manufacturing node against the relevant mechanical, thermal, timing, and handling conditions.
  4. Set decision gates for trials, change control, release, and continuity before throughput pressure becomes the decision-maker.

TPI boundary

Thermodynamic Parity Index (TPI) is a physical engineering diagnostic index used where thermal, mechanical, or process-stress conditions are material to the scale-up question. It tests whether relevant upstream stresses remain aligned with the physical tolerances required at the next manufacturing node. It is not a general logistics, financial, or supplier-scoring label.

Scope boundary

Scale-Up Readiness is not laboratory formulation development, a regulatory approval, or a promise of yield or market success. It is an execution review that clarifies the evidence and operating conditions required for a controlled commercial decision.

Test the next node before it carries the promise.

Start with the product, planned scale step, and execution constraint that require an evidence-led review.
